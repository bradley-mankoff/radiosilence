# Monetization research for Bad Game Warning

Bad Game Warning (repo `/Users/bradley_mankoff/Developer/personal_code/bad_game_warning`, server in `app.py`) is a spoiler-free page that tells a Dallas Mavericks fan whether tonight's game is worth watching from behind, using ESPN's public win-probability endpoints. The owner wants to offer it free first to attract users, then add gambling (sports betting) ads. This file is research for planning, and it gives facts, not legal advice. Consult a gaming lawyer before signing any affiliate deal or running any gambling ad.

## Answer in five sentences

Sportsbook affiliate deals pay per new funded bettor (CPA), as a share of net revenue, or as a hybrid, and joining requires direct approval plus state vendor or ancillary licenses where the operator is live. Gambling ads carry strict placement rules (age 21 plus, no minors, responsible-gaming messaging with 1-800-GAMBLER, geo-targeting to legal states, and platform certification from Google, Apple, and Meta). Live-odds ad units and odds widgets inherently spoil a game because the odds reveal who is winning, so a spoiler-free page can only use static brand creatives kept away from live data. Non-gambling options (programmatic display, streaming affiliates, sponsorships, freemium or tip jar) pay far less per user but impose no gambling compliance burden. Growth for a one-team utility most plausibly comes from team communities, bots, and search, and at 1k monthly users no ad category pays meaningfully, so the early goal should be audience, not revenue.

## 1. Sports betting affiliate programs (CPA, revenue share, requirements)

What the programs are: sportsbooks recruit publishers as affiliates, and the publisher links or advertises the sportsbook and is paid when a referred user registers, deposits, or bets. The three standard payout models are CPA (a one-time bounty per qualifying new depositor), revenue share (a percentage of net gaming revenue from referred users for a period or lifetime), and hybrid (a smaller CPA plus a smaller revenue share). Evidence that CPA is the industry term in this market: the iGB homepage fetched on 2026-10-25 (`https://igamingbusiness.com/`) lists a session titled "How BetMGM Brasil is balancing brand, CPA and growth", which confirms operators and affiliates negotiate around CPA even though it gives no dollar figure.

DraftKings: the affiliates page (`https://www.draftkings.com/affiliates`, fetched 2026-10-25) is a contact form ("If you are interested in the DraftKings affiliate program, please reach out with information on how you would promote DraftKings. An account manager will be in touch") with no public rate card, which means terms are negotiated privately. The same page footer carries responsible-gaming text ("Gambling Problem? Call 1-800-GAMBLER", plus Connecticut and New York helpline text, "Must be at least 18 years or older. Higher age limits may apply in some states"), which confirms affiliates are expected to carry the same helpline and age language. No CPA or revenue-share figure is published there.

FanDuel: `https://affiliates.fanduel.com/` fetched as an empty body in this pass, so no payout or requirement fact could be verified from the primary page. BetMGM: `https://affiliates.betmgm.com/` likewise returned an empty body in this pass, so no primary-source rate is claimed. Caesars: `https://www.caesars.com/affiliates` fetched only a generic hotels and casinos shell in this pass, so no sportsbook affiliate terms were verified. Fanatics: `https://www.fanatics.com/affiliate-program` returned "Access Denied" (Edge denial, reference `18.e68c917...`), so no fact is claimed. bet365: `https://www.bet365affiliates.com/` returned a Cloudflare block ("You have been blocked", Ray ID `a4fede53de1c4d98`), so no fact is claimed.

Typical amounts: no primary affiliate page fetched here publishes a dollar CPA, and the secondary affiliate toplists that normally quote ranges (`https://www.legalsportsreport.com/sports-betting-affiliate-programs/`) returned a Cloudflare block in this pass, so this file does not assert a verified CPA from a citable fetch. For planning only, and to be confirmed with each operator's manager: US sportsbook CPA offers are widely described in affiliate press as roughly 100 to 250 dollars per funded account depending on state and volume, with revenue-share offers roughly 20 to 35 percent of net revenue, and hybrids splitting the difference. Treat those ranges as unverified in this pass because the fetches that would confirm them were blocked.

ESPN BET successor (important for the plan): ESPN BET no longer exists as the counterparty. The Wikipedia page `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_Bet` (fetched 2026-10-25) states the brand was "Defunct November 2025", that "Penn moved ESPN Bet customers to its subsidiary theScore Bet in December 2025", and that "DraftKings entered into an agreement to make it the official sports betting partner of ESPN". Practical effect: do not plan around an "ESPN BET affiliate deal"; the live counterparts are theScore Bet (Penn), DraftKings (now ESPN's official partner), FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, Fanatics, and bet365, each negotiated separately. The page also confirms ESPN BET operated mobile betting in 18 states, which illustrates that even a major brand was state-by-state licensed rather than national.

Licensing to join: states license the operator, and affiliates that market gambling for compensation commonly need a vendor, ancillary, or advertising registration in each state where they promote, with thresholds and fees differing by state. New Jersey: the Division of Gaming Enforcement site (`https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/`, fetched 2026-10-25) now redirects to a new index page, and the older vendor-registration PDF path returned a 404, so the exact current affiliate form and fee were not verified in this pass. Pennsylvania: `https://gaming.pa.gov/` fetched as an empty shell (script-rendered) in this pass, so the exact affiliate registration path was not verified. The planning takeaway is that a small content affiliate should expect to complete at least New Jersey and Pennsylvania vendor or ancillary filings (plus any other launch state) before a sportsbook will pay, and to budget legal time per state, with exact forms to be confirmed with the operator's compliance team and the state portals. No filing is asserted here beyond the existence of the regulators, because the form-level pages were not retrievable by plain fetch.

## 2. Legal and policy limits on gambling ads

This section reports what the platforms publish, not advice. Age, helpline, and geo-targeting requirements recur on every policy page that was retrievable.

US state baseline: states that legalized sports betting after Murphy v. NCAA (2018) generally require bettors to be 21 or older (some daily-fantasy age floors are 18, but sportsbook is 21 in the fetched DraftKings footer language: "Must be at least 18 years or older. Higher age limits may apply in some states"). States require responsible-gaming messaging and the national helpline 1-800-GAMBLER on ads and landing pages, plus state-specific numbers where applicable (the DraftKings footer names Connecticut and New York variants). Ads must be geo-targeted only to states where the operator is licensed, and must not target minors or college audiences. The Wikipedia overview `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_betting_in_the_United_States` (fetched 2026-10-25) confirms the post-2018 state-by-state legalization frame, and the gambling-harm context, but state-by-state ad rules were not enumerated there and must be checked per launch state.

American Gaming Association Responsible Marketing Code for Sports Wagering: the AGA code is the industry standard that member operators follow (age 21 plus audience composition thresholds, no targeting of underage or problem-gambling audiences, responsible-play messaging, no misleading "risk free" claims). The primary text was not verified in this pass because `https://www.americangaming.org/responsible-marketing-code-for-sports-wagering/` and `https://www.americangaming.org/policies/responsibility/` both returned a bot challenge ("Just a moment... Enable JavaScript and cookies to continue"), and the PDF path returned a "Page Not Found" page. Do not quote the code from this file; read the current code from the AGA before drafting any creative.

Google Ads gambling policy: verified. The page `https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6018017?hl=en` (fetched 2026-10-25, title "Gambling and games") states Google "support[s] responsible gambling advertising", requires advertisers to "abide by local gambling laws and industry standards", and requires gambling and gambling-promoting destinations to "only target approved countries, have a landing page that displays information about responsible gambling and never target minors". A companion AdSense policy page (`https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en`, fetched 2026-10-25) confirms publishers must follow program policies and that restricted content draws less advertising, which matters for section 4.

## 3. Product tension: live odds spoil the game

The tension is real and structural. Any live line, spread, moneyline, total, or win-probability widget reveals who is leading, so rendering it on a page whose promise is "the server never sends the score, clock, quarter, or win-probability percentage to the phone" (project rule, enforced in `app.py`) breaks the core guarantee.

How sportsbook ad units show live odds (verified shape): the DraftKings sportsbook homepage (`https://sportsbook.draftkings.com/`, fetched 2026-10-25) renders sport navigation plus odds hubs ("NFL Odds", "NBA Odds", "Live Odds", "Super Bowl Odds", "NBA Finals", "Prop Bets", "Futures") and gates detail behind login ("You must be logged in to view this content"), which confirms the standard pattern of odds-index pages feeding event pages with live prices. Odds-comparison pages make the spoiler mechanism explicit: `https://www.oddsportal.com/` (fetched 2026-10-25) advertises "live score updates with the real-time data available on the live odds page, providing odds comparisons for all matches worldwide as the goals fly in", which is exactly the construct a spoiler-free page must avoid. No odds widget is spoiler-safe while a game is live, because a shortening moneyline is a score in disguise.

Static non-odds creatives do exist. The DraftKings affiliates page itself is one: a brand form with no lines, no prices, and no scores, plus responsible-gaming footer text. Operators routinely supply static banners (brand logo, bonus offer with terms, "21 plus" and helpline lockups) and pre-match content that does not update in play. The safe pattern for this app, if gambling ads are ever added, is static brand or sign-up creatives on pre-game and post-game states only, with no odds, no live data, no scores, and no in-play widgets, and with the ad slot suppressed or replaced by a non-gambling fallback while a game is live. Whether operators will accept a placement that refuses live-odds units is a commercial question for the affiliate manager, and it should be settled before integration because live-odds widgets are the highest-yielding unit the operator will want to serve.

YouTube evidence on neutral labels (reused from highlights research): YouTube oEmbed for the sampled Spurs at Mavericks reel (`https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vTUV7qXzPSA&format=json`, fetched 2026-10-25) returns title "SPURS at MAVERICKS | FULL GAME HIGHLIGHTS | October 24, 2026" with author "NBA", which confirms embeds are enabled but titles name both teams. That is why the app must use neutral labels ("Watch highlights") rather than video titles, and the same discipline applies to betting creatives (neutral brand text, no matchup-plus-price combo that implies a leader).

## 4. Non-gambling monetisation for a small sports utility

Programmatic display (AdSense and equivalents): allowed on sports content, but restricted-content adjacency earns less, and a one-line verdict page has little viewable inventory. The AdSense program-policies page (fetched above) confirms policy compliance is mandatory and restricted content earns less, but no RPM figure is published there. RPM benchmarks from monetization vendors were not verified in this pass because the candidate pages returned 404s (`https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/what-is-a-good-adsense-rpm/` and `https://snigel.com/blog/adsense-rpm-benchmarks/` both returned "Page not found" shells). For planning only, and to be replaced by a measured test: small sports utilities commonly report display RPMs in the low single to low double dollars (roughly 3 to 15 dollars per thousand pageviews depending on season, geography, and viewability), which at 1k monthly users keeping a page open for one game is on the order of a few dollars per month. Treat that range as unverified here.

Carbon Ads: verified as a poor fit. The homepage (`https://www.carbonads.net/`, fetched 2026-10-25) describes "350+ hand-picked tech sites", case studies from developer tools, and an advertiser FAQ stating "Successful first-time advertisers typically start with a $5,000 to $10,000 monthly investment", with no self-serve option. A Mavericks fan utility is not a developer audience, so Carbon is passed over.

EthicalAds: verified as developer-focused and small-site friendly but still the wrong audience. The advertisers page (`https://www.ethicalads.io/advertisers/`, fetched 2026-10-25) publishes CPMs (for example "All developers $3.10" run of network and "$5.25" for US, Canada, UK, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland) with a "$1,000 minimum ad buy". The network is developer documentation sites, so it is not a sports-content buyer, and the minimums exceed what 1k monthly users can justify.

Streaming affiliates (YouTube TV, NBA League Pass, Fubo): the products fit the audience (watching from behind still needs a way to watch), but the affiliate paths were only partly verified. NBA League Pass pricing is verified (`https://www.nba.com/league-pass`, fetched 2026-10-25): "$109.99 / SEASON" for League Pass and "$159.99 / SEASON" for Premium, with blackout rules and up to 3 devices on Premium. No affiliate commission rate is published there, so revenue per referral is unverified. Fubo's site confirms an affiliate program exists in principle (the 404 page for `https://www.fubo.tv/welcome/affiliates` still renders a footer link "Become an affiliate"), but the terms page (`https://www.fubo.tv/legal/affiliate-terms`) fetched empty, so no commission is claimed. A YouTube TV affiliate listing on PartnerStack (`https://partnerstack.com/marketplace/youtube-tv-affiliate-program`) returned "Not Found", so no YouTube TV affiliate fact is claimed. Expect streaming affiliates to pay CPA bounties per trial or subscription (order of tens of dollars), to be confirmed with each program, and note League Pass blackouts limit its value to out-of-market fans.

Sponsorships and team or league partnerships: no rate verified in this pass. A one-team utility can sell a season presenting sponsorship to a local advertiser (bar, ticket exchange, apparel shop) more easily than it can earn programmatic revenue, but pricing is negotiated and inventory is tiny (one verdict per game day). League or team partnerships were not verified: NBA.com legal and help pages were bot-walled in the prior highlights pass, and no partnership rate is published on the League Pass page.

Freemium and tip jar: no platform fee verified here beyond the hosting file's cost model (Cloud Run free tier covers roughly the first 1k to 2k active users at zero cost; Fly.io about 2 to 4 dollars per month; see `docs/research/hosting-license-install.md`). At that cost base, a voluntary tip (for example 3 dollars per season from 2 percent of 1k users) covers hosting, while a paywall for verdicts would destroy the free-growth loop the owner wants. No tip-platform fee is asserted here.

Revenue per 1k monthly users (planning sketch, not a promise): display ads at single-digit RPMs yield single-digit dollars per month at 1k users; one streaming trial bounty per month yields tens of dollars; one local sponsorship yields hundreds per season if sold; gambling CPA would yield hundreds per funded bettor but requires the licensing and compliance burden in section 2 plus the spoiler-safe creative restriction in section 3. The honest planning line is that 1k monthly users pays for hosting and little else under any non-gambling model.
