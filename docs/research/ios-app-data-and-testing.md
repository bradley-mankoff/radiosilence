# Combined findings: where RadioSilence's iOS app may get live NBA data, and how to test it without paying Apple

Two researchers answered the same two questions on 2026-11-20 for epic `bad_game_warning-a8f` ("iOS app: free, no ads", a hosted standard-library Python server judging NBA games, a native Swift app with notifications and a widget, no ads, eventually the public App Store). This file is the merged account: the answer first, then where the two agree, where they disagree and whose evidence is stronger, and what nobody has settled. The two source files are kept with Archon Run 0a286d16 (`findings-a.md`, `findings-b.md`); prior research they build on is `docs/research/data-sources-and-scaling.md` (2026-10-25) and `docs/research/own-win-probability-model.md` (2026-10-25).

## The answer

Buy balldontlie's GOAT plan for the NBA at 39.99 USD a month (383.90 a year) when the app is meant for anyone but the Boss: it is the only self-serve feed that carries live score, clock, play-by-play and spreads from one vendor, and its terms of service grant display, distribution and derivative works in plain words, while ESPN's and the NBA's own terms forbid the use outright. Before that purchase a $0 prototype is possible by splitting suppliers — balldontlie's free tier for live score and clock plus The Odds API's free 500 credits a month for the pre-game spread — but it carries no play-by-play, so the catch-up box score cannot ship on it. No route needs a licensed win-probability feed, because the repo's three-input model reproduces ESPN's percentage to about 2.4 points and agrees on 40 of 41 Stay-away calls, provided its constants are refitted on licensed data first, since today they were fitted on ESPN summaries that Disney's terms bar from being collected. A free Apple ID gives 7-day installs on up to 3 devices and 3 apps per device, with widgets, App Groups and background refresh working and remote push and TestFlight not, so a notification-driven build cannot reach a friend's phone without the 99 USD. Android is the cheap test bed: 25 USD once for Play Console, internal testing for 100 testers in minutes with no review, free Firebase App Distribution, and plain APK sideloading unimpeded in the United States through 2026.

## Where the two agree

### Data

Both researchers reached the same verdict on every feed they could read, and the reason is legal rather than technical: a score is a fact, but the contract that delivers it forbids what a public app does. Apple's App Store Review Guideline 5.2.2 turns that contract into a review gate — "If your app uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service, ensure that you are specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request" (`https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/`) — so a feed that forbids redistribution is not merely a civil risk.

| Feed | Price both verified | What is live | Verdict for a free public app |
|---|---|---|---|
| balldontlie GOAT | 39.99 USD/mo per league, 383.90/yr; 7-day trial at 5 req/min | Live box scores, play-by-play from the 2025 season, live odds with `spread_home` | **Yes.** Terms allow display, distribution and derivative works. Win probability is post-game only |
| balldontlie Free | 0 USD, 5 req/min | Says Games only; the `NBAGame` schema carries `period`, `time` and scores | Yes by the same terms, but no plays and no odds |
| The Odds API | Starter 0 USD, 500 credits/mo; 30 USD for 20,000 | Spreads every 60 s pre-match and 40 s in-play | **Yes**, derived values expressly permitted; scores have no clock |
| Sportradar | 30-day trial, 1,000 requests per rolling 30 days; production sales-only | Scores, play-by-play with an explicit `possession` field, odds | Trial: no, evaluation only. Production: contract only |
| SportsDataIO | No public price | Live scores, game state, play-by-play, in-play odds | Trial returns "scrambled data"; production needs a commercial discussion |
| MySportsFeeds | 29/mo commercial, 5/mo personal, both "CORE feeds and Non-Live access" | Play-by-play only in the DETAILS add-on; real-time tier unpriced | Not without an email; the cheap tier is not live |
| API-Sports | A: unreadable. B: 0 USD at 100 requests/day, paid from 10 USD/mo, via Wayback | Score and clock every 15 s, odds; **no play-by-play** | No, for lack of plays |
| ESPN (`site.api.espn.com`) | Free, keyless | Everything, including win probability per play | **No.** Disney's licence is "personal, noncommercial use only" and bans automated extraction |
| NBA (`cdn.nba.com`, `stats.nba.com`) | Free, blocked | Play-by-play, scoreboard, `winprobabilitypbp` | **No.** 403 and timeout on 2026-11-20, and the terms name this exact use |

The two decisive quotes are agreed. Disney's Terms of Use (`https://disneytermsofuse.com/english/`) grant use "for your personal, noncommercial use only ... with no right to reproduce, distribute, communicate to the public, make available to the public, or transform any Disney Product", and bar anyone who would "access, monitor, copy or extract the Disney Products using a robot, spider, script, or other automated means ... or otherwise compiling, building, creating or contributing to any collection of data, data set or database". NBA.com's Terms of Use, section 9 (`https://www.nba.com/termsofuse`, updated 2026-07-13), limits NBA Statistics to "legitimate news reporting or private, non-commercial purposes" and adds "(vi) the NBA Statistics may not be used in connection with any product or service that presents a live, near-live, or other real-time or archived play-by-play account or depiction of any NBA game". Neither clause turns on price, so making the app free does not move it inside either licence; the public page and the phone app are different legal objects.

Both also agree that the feeds which might be licensed are gated behind a sales conversation, and that their trials cannot stand in for a product: Sportradar's 30 days and 1,000 requests is less than one team's game polled every 30 seconds for three hours (about 360 calls), and SportsDataIO's trial returns scrambled bytes.

Both agree the phone never touches a feed. Judging stays in the Python server, which also keeps the spoiler rule intact, and the app receives one word per team.

Both agree that computing win probability ourselves removes the need to license a percentage, and does not remove the need to license score, clock and spread. The measured ablation in `docs/research/own-win-probability-model.md` is 5.3 points of mean error from lead and clock alone, 2.4 once the pre-game spread is added, 2.35 with a possession bump; at the 95 percent line the model agrees with ESPN in 40 of 41 test-half games with zero false alarms, and on a full-season replay it fires in 40 games where ESPN fires in 42. Both therefore read balldontlie's post-game-only win probability as a non-problem: what the app wants from that vendor is plays and odds.

### Testing

Both read Apple's comparison page (`https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/`) and got identical numbers for a Personal Team: "You can register up to 10 App IDs, which expire after 7 days", "up to 3 devices, which expire after 7 days", "up to 3 apps per device", and profiles that "will expire 7 days from issuance", after which the app must be rebuilt and reinstalled.

Both place the same capabilities on each side of the line, from Apple's capability table (`https://developer.apple.com/help/account/reference/supported-capabilities-ios/`) and, independently, from Xcode 27.2's own cached portal catalogue. Available free: App Groups, Background modes, Data protection. Not available free: Push notifications, Time Sensitive Notifications, Communication Notifications, Associated Domains, iCloud, In-App Purchase. Both conclude that a WidgetKit widget works — it needs no entitlement of its own — that background refresh works, that local notifications can be scheduled without any entitlement, and that remote push cannot be built at all, which is the one feature in the grilling answers that the free tier cannot exercise.

Both confirm that TestFlight, App Store Connect, Ad Hoc and Xcode Cloud are member-only rows, and that the Program is "99 USD per membership year" (`https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/`). Testers: up to 10,000 external and 100 internal, builds live 90 days (`https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/testflight-overview/`). The fee waiver exists but is closed to individuals: A quotes the eligible applicant as "a nonprofit organization, accredited educational institution, or government entity"; B adds the exclusion "Not be an individual, sole proprietor, or single-person business" (`https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/fee-waivers/`).

Both end at the same practical point: before the 99 USD, the only zero-friction way to put the Verdict in front of a friend is the Home Screen web page the repo already serves, and the only free path onto an iPhone is a cable, a 7-day clock and a device slot.

On Android both find the same shape: 25 USD once (`https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435`, "There is a US$25 one-time registration fee"), internal testing for up to 100 testers with builds arriving in minutes and no standard review (`.../answer/9845334`, `.../answer/14151465`), Firebase App Distribution at no cost (`https://firebase.google.com/pricing`), and developer verification beginning "September 30, 2026" for installs "from participating stores in select regions" — Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand — with the global rollout in 2027, ADB installs permanently exempt, and a free limited-distribution account that needs no government ID (`https://developer.android.com/developer-verification`, `.../guides/faq`).

## Where they disagree, and whose evidence is stronger

**1. Whether the cheapest clean route costs $0 or 39.99 USD a month.** A concludes balldontlie GOAT is the one purchase, because it is "the only self-serve feed found that both carries live score, clock, play-by-play and spreads and says in plain words that a free or paid app may display derived values" — a claim that is correctly scoped to the combination and stands. B goes further and separates the suppliers: balldontlie's free tier for live score and clock, The Odds API's free 500 credits for the spread, the own model for the percentage. B's evidence is a reading of the published OpenAPI schema (`https://www.balldontlie.io/openapi/nba.yml`), where `NBAGame` carries `period`, `time`, `status_state` and both scores, against A's reading of the tier table, where Free lists "Teams, Players, Games only". Both are right and they are describing different things: the free tier lacks plays and odds, but the Games endpoint it does include appears to carry a live clock. B's addition therefore holds, with one caveat nobody tested — no request was made with a free key, so the free tier's live behaviour is inferred from the schema. The choice between the $0 and $39.99 routes is not a difference of fact but of scope: the $0 route cannot serve `/catchup`, because catch-up is built from substitution plays, and play-by-play is the one field it lacks. If catch-up ships with the phone app, GOAT stops being optional.

**2. Whether Sportradar's licence text can be read before signing.** B says it cannot: "Sportradar publishes no API licence terms on its website (`https://sportradar.com/terms-and-conditions/` and `/legal/` both 404)" — and marks redistribution "not public; contract-only". A quotes the text, from a different host: `https://developer.sportradar.com/terms-and-conditions`, section 3.1, "Customer is not authorized, and agrees not to, use the Products in connection with any commercial use or any use involving publication or display of the Data or Content in any form or media", and section 2.2, where the paid licence grants distribution "on the Properties" and then forbids the customer to "(i) copy, modify or make derivative works of the Products". A's evidence is stronger: it is the developer portal's own terms, quoted with section numbers, where B only hit 404s on the corporate marketing site's legal pages. The consequence matters more than the citation: the trial is unusable for anything the public sees, and the paid licence's derivative-works bar is exactly the clause a computed win probability would trip, because the verdict is a function of their play-by-play. B missed that clause and therefore rates Sportradar's route more cleanly than it is. B is right on the part A does not contest: production price is unpublished, and the Probabilities and Live Odds products cannot be trialled self-serve.

**3. API-Sports: unknown, or knowable through the archive.** A could not read the site at all — `https://api-sports.io/pricing` returned HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare challenge on both 2026-10-25 and 2026-11-20, and A's conclusion is "Unknown. Do not plan on it without reading the site by hand." B also failed live but recovered the facts from Wayback snapshots: 0 USD at 100 requests a day, paid tiers from about 10 USD a month, "Real-time games and events updated every 15 seconds", prices in the range 19 to 39 USD at other tiers, and a Basketball v1 endpoint list with `games`, `games/statistics/*`, `odds` and `bookmakers` but no plays endpoint. Technically B's evidence is stronger because it produced facts where A produced a wall, but its provenance is weaker — a 2026-02-01 snapshot of the football pricing page and a 2026-10-31 snapshot of the homepage, with the basketball product possibly priced apart. Read together, the conclusion is B's and it is firmer than either alone: API-Sports could supply score, clock and odds cheaply, and cannot supply play-by-play, which is a better reason to drop it than "the site would not load".

**4. How sure to be about the widget.** A says "the widget works", citing the capability table's free column and Xcode's catalogue. B says "very likely, verify with a 20-minute build", on the same two sources, because no widget has been built and installed on a physical device from a free team. B's calibration is better: both sources establish that no entitlement blocks a widget, and neither is a test. A's own design note reduces the risk A is confident about — if the widget fetches the Verdict from the hosted server in its timeline provider, it needs no App Group at all, so the only unproven step is extension provisioning under a free team, not shared-container provisioning. B's one extra caution is worth keeping: Apple's web table and Xcode's catalogue disagree about Game Center, which is a reminder to treat the catalogue as supporting evidence and Apple's table as governing.

**5. Whether the model's training data is a problem.** A raises it and B does not, and it is the more serious of the two findings. The shipped constants were fitted by `docs/research/own_model.py` on 82 ESPN summaries, reading `summary["winprobability"]` and `summary["pickcenter"]`, and `docs/research/backtest.py` builds exactly the kind of cached dataset Disney's automation clause forbids. The numbers themselves are three constants, but the answer to "where did these come from" would be uncomfortable at App Review under 5.2.2. A's fix is cheap and is permitted by the vendor: balldontlie's terms allow "model training, evaluation and backtesting" and let a customer "retain and continue using that Data and your derived works and models after your subscription or access ends" (`https://www.balldontlie.io/terms`), so one or two months of GOAT buys a larger and cleaner fit set.

One point neither file makes, worth pricing before the refit: fitting against balldontlie's post-game win-probability timelines makes the model imitate a second vendor's model rather than ESPN's, which settles provenance but not independence. Fitting the same three-input form against actual game outcomes — a fact, needing no percentage feed from anyone — would remove the derivative question entirely, at the cost of a new fit and a new round of validation against the 40-of-41 benchmark. That is a modelling decision for the Map, not a blocker.

**6. The state of this Mac.** A writes that "signing in with a free Apple ID in Xcode is all that is missing", on the evidence of one development certificate (an Apple Development certificate in the Boss's personal name) and no `.mobileprovision` files. B read Xcode's own preferences and found a Personal Team already configured — `isFreeProvisioningTeam = 1`, the Boss's personal team — plus an iOS 27.0 simulator and still no provisioning profiles. B's evidence is stronger and it changes the first step: nothing needs signing in, and the first real-device install starts the 7-day clock. Both agree no device has been registered.

**7. Small factual splits.** MySportsFeeds is quoted as "29 USD" by A and "CAD$29" by B from the same pricing page; the currency is unresolved and does not change the verdict, since the tier either quotes is non-live. The Ad Hoc device cap is "not verified" in A and verified in B at 100 devices per product family per membership year (`https://developer.apple.com/help/account/devices/devices-overview/`), so B is stronger. The Android limited-distribution account is 20 devices in A; B reports 20 and 50 on two Google pages and recommends planning on 20, which is the better answer because it is the smaller number and it names the contradiction.

## What is still unknown

* **balldontlie's live cadence.** The docs never say how often `/v1/plays` or `box_scores/live` refresh during a game, and every documented example is a finished game. One evening of the 7-day trial with a paid key settles it, and it decides whether the 60-second page refresh and the 30-second verdict window are achievable.
* **Whether balldontlie's free tier really returns a running clock and period.** Inferred from the `NBAGame` schema, not observed; a free key and one live game answers it.
* **balldontlie's odds coverage.** Whether every NBA game carries a spread before tip-off, how many books, and how early the first line appears. The model's fallback is a toss-up prior, which `docs/research/own-win-probability-model.md` measured at 18 of 82 games in the old data — a coverage gap is a verdict-quality gap.
* **Whether balldontlie's post-game win-probability timelines cover 2025-26 densely enough to refit on.** The endpoint exists from 2018-19 and "coverage varies".
* **Sportradar's production price, and whether a free public app is licensable at all**, plus whether a computed win probability survives clause 2.2(i). Nothing public answers either; both need a sales conversation with the clause in hand.
* **SportsDataIO's price, and whether its Replay dataset would let the verdict be backtested offline.**
* **MySportsFeeds' real-time price, the currency of its quoted prices, and its API licence**: the docs sit behind a login and the terms page covers the website, not the feed.
* **API-Sports' current prices and terms**, since only Wayback snapshots could be read, and whether its basketball product has live odds at all.
* **Whether the league would object to a distributor-fed verdict app.** Buying from balldontlie converts an NBA-terms problem into a supplier-terms problem, and balldontlie says so itself: its section 11 permission "applies only to rights we can grant and is not legal clearance from any third party". What the app publishes is one word per team computed from facts, a far smaller footprint than the "live play-by-play account" and "comprehensive, regularly updated statistics database" that NBA clauses (vi) and (vii) target — but that is a lawyer's argument, not a licence, and should be recorded as such.
* **Whether Apple's Guideline 5.2.1 exposure** — team names, no logos, no claim of official status — is acceptable to App Review. No case was tested.
* **Whether a widget installed by a free personal team actually renders on a physical iPhone**, and whether App Groups provisions cleanly in Xcode 27.2 for a free team. Both sources say the capability is allowed; no device has run either.
* **Whether Apple keeps the 7-day, 3-device, 10-App-ID numbers.** They are current as of the 2026-11-20 fetch and Apple changes them quietly.
* **Whether Google's limited-distribution account is 20 devices or 50.** Google's own pages disagree; plan on 20.
* **The Play closed-test gate**, which B found and A did not: personal accounts created after 2023-11-13 must run a closed test with "a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days" before production access (`https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465`). It does not affect internal testing, so it is a fact about release, not about the beta.

The single check that would change the most is the cheapest: one live NBA game in the GOAT trial, watched with a clock beside the API's `time` and `wallclock` fields, settles cadence, clock behaviour and odds coverage at once, and decides whether the shipped app is a 39.99 USD-a-month product or a $0 one without catch-up.
