# An own win-probability model for the 95% Stay away rule

Date: 2026-10-25. Script: `docs/research/own_model.py` (run `python3 docs/research/own_model.py /tmp/bgw-season-2026`). Data: the same 82 cached 2025-26 Mavericks summaries used in `docs/research/backtest-2025-26.md` (26 wins, 56 losses), replayed in chronological play order.

## Answer

A three-input Stern-style probit model over home lead, seconds left, and the pre-game spread as a prior, plus a half-point bump for possession, reproduces ESPN's in-window Readings to a mean absolute error of 2.35 percentage points on the second half of the season after fitting on the first half. At the 95% Stay away line it agrees with ESPN in 40 of 41 test-half games: it fires in 24 of the 25 games ESPN calls before the last 2:00, never fires when ESPN stays silent, and its first call lags ESPN's by a median of under a minute. The spread does almost all the work, cutting test error from 5.3 points (lead and clock alone) to 2.4, while the possession heuristic shaves only another 0.1 because ESPN publishes no possession flag and it must be inferred from play types. The model's only full-season misses are close Dallas losses by 4 and 6 points, the games where a Stay away call is most questionable anyway, and it never misses a genuine blowout or invents a call ESPN never made. A licensed play-by-play feed carrying score, clock, and possession, plus any published spread, can therefore drive the 95% rule with barely changed behavior, so ESPN's win probability is replaceable whenever the terms require it.

## Method

Each cached summary contributes one training row per win-probability reading: the home lead and game clock from the joined play, the seconds remaining (regulation plus the current overtime period, treated as the last one), the pre-game spread from the summary's `pickcenter` array converted to a home expected margin (the spread is quoted from the home side, so a spread of minus 8.5 for home favorite GS means a home prior of plus 8.5), and possession inferred from play types because ESPN publishes no possession flag. The possession heuristic walks plays in chronological order: rebounds and steals go to the credited team, turnovers, violations, and fouls give the ball to the other side, a made basket gives the ball to the side that was scored on, only a made final free throw changes possession, and period breaks reset to unknown since the alternating-possession arrow is not in the feed. Readings are sorted by sequence number with the wallclock as tiebreak before replaying, because ESPN's arrays are not strictly chronological (corrections land out of order). Caveat added 2026-10-25: late stat corrections carry higher sequence numbers than the plays around them (see `backtest-2025-26.md`), so game-time order (period, then clock), which `app.py` now uses, is the better key; the few misplaced corrections barely move a fit over about 40,000 Readings. The model is P(home win) = Phi((lead + w * prior * t/2880 + v * poss) / (sigma * sqrt(t))), the classic Brownian-motion form with a drift term from the spread and an additive possession bump. Parameters come from grid search on mean log-loss over the first 41 games of the season (by date) and every number below labeled test comes from the last 41 games.

## How close the Readings are

| Variant | Fit log-loss | Test MAE (in-window Readings) |
|---|---|---|
| A: lead and clock only (sigma 0.33) | 0.4325 | 0.0528 |
| B: plus pre-game spread (sigma 0.33, weight 0.75) | 0.4291 | 0.0241 |
| C: plus possession (sigma 0.33, weight 0.75, bump 0.5) | 0.4281 | 0.0235 |

The spread is the whole story after the score and clock: without it the model fires a median of 5 minutes late and misses blowouts involving big underdogs (for example the 2025-10-28 home game against OKC, where Dallas was a 16.5-point underdog). Possession helps only slightly, which is expected from an inferred flag that is wrong around period breaks and and-one sequences. Error by game stage with the recommended deployment params (sigma 0.33, spread weight 1.0, possession 0.5): 2nd quarter 3.62 points over 5,325 Readings, 3rd quarter 2.65 over 9,752, 4th quarter 2.26 over 10,437, overtime 4.46 over 372. The model runs a touch cooler on the home side than ESPN early (bias minus 0.85 points in the 2nd quarter, shrinking to minus 0.15 in the 4th), which is why most of its first calls lag ESPN's rather than lead.

## How the 95% calls compare

Honest split-half evaluation with the fit-half params on the last 41 games: ESPN calls Stay away before the last 2:00 in 25 games and the model in 24. The single ESPN-only game is 2026-03-24 against Golden State (Dallas lost by 6, ESPN first crossed with 5.3 minutes left). There are zero model-only games. Among the 24 games both call, the model's first call lags ESPN's by a median of 0.6 minutes and a mean of 2.7, with the worst lag 13.7 minutes in the 2026-04-11 San Antonio loss (Dallas down 19, ESPN fired with 28.5 left, the model at 15.2).

Full-season replay with the recommended deployment params (spread weight 1.0, which tests slightly better than the fit-half 0.75 at 2.19 versus 2.35 points of test MAE): ESPN fires in 42 games and the model in 40, with 2 misses, 0 false alarms, and a first-call lag of median 0.0 and mean plus 1.0 minutes. The two misses are 2025-11-25 against Miami (lost by 4, one of the backtest's wrongly called games) and the same Golden State game. The largest lags all run in the safe direction on games Dallas lost by 19 or more, except 2026-02-01 against Houston (lost by 4), where the model's 9.8-minute delay spared viewers a Stay away call on a game that came down to the wire. One game runs the other way: 2026-03-04 against Charlotte (lost by 27), where the model fires 5.9 minutes before ESPN. Chronological replay gives the same ESPN total as the backtest's array-order replay (42 games), so the ordering fix changes no headline count.

## What a licensed replacement feed must carry

Score and clock are nonnegotiable and every licensed play-by-play feed has them. Possession is worth about 0.1 points of MAE, so it can be inferred or even dropped, but a feed with an explicit possession flag (the NBA stats backend's `HOME_POSS_IND`, per `docs/research/data-sources-and-scaling.md`) removes the heuristic entirely. The spread matters far more than possession: 18 of the 82 cached summaries (22 percent) carry no `pickcenter` entry and fall back to a toss-up prior, so a live service needs a spread source with full coverage, which a licensed odds feed would provide. One caveat: the cached spreads are closing lines captured after the season, not the line at tipoff, so the fitted spread weight of 0.75 to 1.0 slightly overstates what a tipoff line is worth. A second caveat: the model is trained to imitate ESPN, so it inherits ESPN's modeling choices and any ESPN error; it is a replacement for the feed, not a better model of who will win.

## Published NBA win-probability models

Three public points of comparison, all converging on the same inputs (score, time, prior strength, possession):

* Hal S. Stern (1994), "A Brownian Motion Model for the Progress of Sports Scores," Journal of the American Statistical Association 89(427), pages 1128 to 1134, https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476872. The ancestor of the model above: the score difference follows Brownian motion with drift, so the win probability is a Normal CDF in the lead scaled by the square root of time left. Stern estimated one league-wide drift and volatility; the model here adds a per-game drift from the betting spread.
* inpredictable's NBA win-probability calculator, https://stats.inpredictable.com/nba/wpCalc.php. Its inputs are the point spread, the score difference, the time remaining, and which team has possession, fit by regression over historical play-by-play. Our ablation confirms its input ordering: spread first, possession last.
* Luke Benz (lbenz730 on GitHub, https://github.com/lbenz730, author of the ncaahoopR package) publishes NBA win-probability modeling code and writeups, fitting richer regressions and gradient-boosted models over play-by-play features including team strength and rest. That is the natural next step if a linear probit ever proves too coarse: same feed, heavier model.

## Limitations

The verdict sample is one bad Mavericks season (26 wins), so most Stay away calls are Dallas getting blown out and almost none test the model on Dallas blowing someone out. Overtime Readings are the worst fit (4.46 points) because the model assumes the current overtime is the last. The possession heuristic is unverified play by play; its small measured value may reflect inference noise rather than the true value of knowing who has the ball. Reproduce everything with `python3 docs/research/own_model.py /tmp/bgw-season-2026`, which prints the variant table and the split-half Stay away agreement in about two seconds.
